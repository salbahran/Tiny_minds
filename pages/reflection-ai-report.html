What we built

Tiny Brains is a learning website for children aged 2 to 6. Children explore three learning worlds (numbers, the alphabet and animals) through big colourful cards, sounds and simple games. Parents can create an account and log in from the home page.

For children

Large buttons, bright colours, friendly characters and audio, so even children who cannot read yet can play.

For parents

A clear overview of what children learn, plus a simple sign-up and log-in.

For the course

Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript, a responsive layout, and documentation pages for planning, design and AI use.

Who did what

We split the website by page, so each member owned one page from sketch to finished code. Shared parts (header, footer, colours and fonts) were agreed together so every page looks like the same website.

Team collaboration experience

At the beginning of the project, we clearly discussed and assigned each team member's roles and responsibilities, ensuring that everyone understood their tasks from the start. This helped the collaboration process run smoothly throughout the project. We consistently supported one another, shared ideas, discussed challenges, and reviewed each other's work to ensure accuracy and maintain consistency across all project components. We also maintained open communication and worked together to solve problems whenever they arose, which helped us stay organised and work effectively as a team.

How we worked

We followed the same steps for every page:

  1. PlanAgreed on the audience, the four main pages and who owns each one.
  2. SketchDrew each page by hand on a tablet to decide the layout.
  3. AI designGave the sketches to ChatGPT to turn them into colourful mockups.
  4. BuildWrote the HTML and CSS ourselves, using the mockups as a guide.
  5. Add sound & playAdded audio and card interactions with JavaScript.
  6. Test & fixChecked every page on desktop, tablet and phone and fixed problems.

How we used AI

AI was our assistant, not our builder. It turned ideas into pictures and helped with small parts of the code, but we wrote and checked the website ourselves. The full list of tools and prompts is in the AI plan on the Overview page.

What AI did

  • Turned our sketches into mockups (ChatGPT)
  • Suggested the palette and drew the characters (ChatGPT)
  • Made the letter cards and cubes (Canva AI)
  • Read out the names of letters and animals (Telnyx text-to-speech)
  • Helped with sound and card-flip JavaScript (Claude)

What we did

  • Planned the website and drew every sketch
  • Wrote the HTML and CSS by hand
  • Tested and changed the AI code to fit our pages
  • Listened to each recording and matched it to its card
  • Made the pages responsive and accessible

Reflection

What went well

Sketching first meant we agreed on the layout before writing code. Splitting the site by page let everyone work at the same time without breaking each other's files.

Challenges

  • Keeping four pages consistent was hard.
  • AI images did not always match our layout.
  • AI code did not always fit our pages.
  • Matching audio to the right card took time.
  • Making every page work on all screens.

What we learned

  • We learned to give and accept feedback, meet deadlines and ask for help instead of staying stuck.
Technical outcomes
  • AI gave us quick ideas and visuals, but every output had to be reviewed, tested and adapted before we used it.
  • Designing for ages 2 to 6 means big buttons, sound and very little text.
  • Drawing wireframes before writing code kept our pages consistent and everyone on the same page.
  • Small differences in class names and file paths caused layout problems, and GitHub helped us keep our work together and match our code.
